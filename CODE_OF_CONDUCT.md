# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume good faith, disagree about the work rather than about each other, and
accept that the maintainer has limited time and may say no.

## Scope

This applies to everything in this project's spaces — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit
messages and code review — and to public behaviour elsewhere that is plausibly connected to the
project, such as representing it at an event.

## Expected behaviour

- Give people the benefit of the doubt. Most friction in a text-only medium is tone, not intent.
- Keep criticism about the code, the design or the decision. "This breaks X because Y" is useful;
  "this is amateurish" is not.
- Accept a decision once it is made, even if you argued the other way. You are welcome to revisit it
  with new information.
- Respect that a maintainer declining a feature, a pull request or a support request is a legitimate
  answer and not a slight.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained disruption of discussion.
- Personal attacks, or comments demeaning someone on the basis of who they are — including race,
  gender, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, disability, age, body size, appearance,
  nationality, religion, or any comparable characteristic.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Deliberately submitting malicious code, or misrepresenting the origin or licensing of a contribution.

## Reporting

Report a concern privately to the maintainer through
[GitHub](https://github.com/szolkowski). Include what happened, where, and links if you have them.

Reports are read by the maintainer, who is also the person who acts on them — a single-maintainer
project has no committee, and pretending otherwise would be misleading. If your report concerns the
maintainer's own conduct and you would rather not raise it with them directly, GitHub's own
[abuse reporting](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse) is the escalation path that does not go
through this project.

You will get a response within a few days. Reports are kept confidential to the extent that acting on
them allows.

## Enforcement

Depending on severity, a response may be a private word, a public correction, editing or removing the
offending content, or a temporary or permanent block from the project's spaces. Retaliation against
someone for reporting in good faith is itself a violation.
